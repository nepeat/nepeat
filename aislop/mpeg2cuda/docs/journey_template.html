mpeg2_cuda journey

An MPEG-2 encoder inside FFmpeg that runs entirely on the GPU, built in two days to feed 1080i transport streams into a Blonder Tongue EQAM for TVs at home. Every number below was measured; every milestone is a commit.

Frames per second, milestone by milestone

Each commit rebuilt from its own worktree and benchmarked back to back: encoder only, 60 frames preloaded in GPU memory, 6000 frames per instance, best of two. Dashed lines are the CPU's mpeg2video on the same clip with matched settings.

Milestones 1–9 ran on an idle GPU. Milestones 10–16 ran later the same day with the live test channels on air; milestone 9 re-measured alongside them came out 2–4% lower than before, so 10–16 read slightly low. From milestone 9 to 16, one park_joy stream went from 3323 to 5229 fps without B pictures and from 2218 to 3279 with them.

Scope

Whole GPU = 4 encoder instances; whole CPU = 8 processes × 4 threads. On touchdown, milestone 8 jumps because progressive sources are now coded as progressive frames, which skips field motion search; interlaced park_joy stays put, as it should. Four instances gain less from milestones 11–16 than one stream does: several instances already kept the GPU busy in each other's gaps.

    Where the GPU time goes

    Per-kernel time from Nsight Systems, park_joy 1080i, I/P. Phase 1 spent two thirds of every picture in motion refinement doing byte loads; packed-byte SAD and one candidate per lane took it from 433 to 54 µs. Milestone 6 moved the coefficient VLC into the transform kernel and dropped a 6.3 MB per-picture buffer.

    The black line is the wall time per picture for one stream. Up to milestone 10 it sits above the bars: the GPU waited on the host between pictures. Milestone 11 queues the next search before the last picture is finished, and from milestone 15 the bitstream kernels and the frame copy run on their own streams beside the search. They then share the GPU, so each kernel takes longer, and the line drops below the bars.

    Baselines

    Before any GPU encoder existed: NVDEC decode with the CPU encoder, and an all-CPU pipeline, H.264 1080p in, MPEG-2 1080i + AC-3 out, live.

    CPU encoder only, re-measured on 2026-10-16. An earlier harness fed frames tagged as intra pictures and made the CPU look about three times faster; these numbers supersede it.

    All the numbers

    Frames per second per milestone, both clips, one stream / four instances, and GPU kernel time per picture.

    Bugs, and what caught them

    The encoder's reconstruction is checked bit for bit against FFmpeg's decoder on every test, and an independent syntax checker parses every slice. Several of these were measurement bugs, not encoder bugs.

    On the air

    Six looping test channels (900 TESTCAT through 905 TSTFISH) run as NVDEC → mpeg2_cuda + AC-3 → MPEG-TS over UDP into the EQAM. A stress run of 6 channels × 4 copies held 24 live 1080i streams at 1.01× realtime with about 1.3% CPU; NVDEC, not the MPEG-2 encoder, is the ceiling for H.264 sources. The live senders were paused from 01:27 to 01:38 for the milestone benchmarks.

    Prior art: GPU pieces of MPEG-2 encoders appear in the literature (Montero et al., 2010–2013) and Elemental shipped closed GPU appliances; no open, fully on-GPU, decoder-bit-exact, interlace-aware MPEG-2 encoder was found. Details in docs/prior_art.md.
